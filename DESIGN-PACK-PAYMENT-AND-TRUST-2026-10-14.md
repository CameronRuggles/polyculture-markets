# Payment, Order & Trust Design — Consolidated Review Pack

**For external review (Claude / ChatGPT). Self-contained: no other document is required.**
**Date: 2026-10-14 · Status: owner-reviewed draft, NOT yet ratified as a spec amendment**

---

## 0. What we need from you

This pack consolidates a payment/order design for a privacy-first,
noncustodial Monero marketplace, after two rounds of external review
and a round of owner attacks. We want:

1. A verdict on each numbered decision (**P** = protocol, **T** = trust,
   **U** = usability): adopt / adopt with changes / reject, with reasons.
2. Any attack we have not modelled — especially attacks that make the
   system *claim* something false, not merely attacks that cost someone
   money.
3. A usability judgement: where does the ordinary (non-expert) user
   fail, abandon, or silently lose protection?
4. Corrections to any factual claim about Monero or OpenPGP mechanics.

Hard rule for this review: **simplicity is a security property here.**
If a step is tedious, users will route around it, and the routed-around
version is the real protocol. Judge every mechanism by whether a
normal person completes it on a normal day.

---

## 1. Setting and non-negotiable constraints

- A marketplace (Rust/Axum/PostgreSQL, no-JS web UI, AGPL) where buyers
  and sellers trade physical/digital goods for Monero.
- **Noncustodial, direct payment:** money moves wallet-to-wallet. The
  platform never holds trade funds, never holds wallet keys, and has
  no escrow or multisig in this version.
- **Ciphertext-only messaging** (OpenPGP) is already the platform's
  communication layer.
- Sellers upload **signed pools of subaddresses** (a subaddress cannot
  be derived from a public view key — derivation needs the private
  view key — so per-invoice addresses are pre-generated by the seller
  and uploaded as a signed list).
- The operator is a small team. Any design requiring large moderation
  staff fails operationally.
- Target users are Monero users: they have a wallet and can make a
  payment, but they are **not** assumed to be PGP experts.

Development status (for stakes): the platform's foundations (accounts,
listings, key records, messaging envelope validation) are built and
have passed independent adversarial test gates. The server-side order
system built earlier assumed platform-visible orders; **adopting this
design supersedes that order model** — server-side order records become
routing metadata (see §4), and order content moves to the user side.
That rework is accepted if the design holds.

---

## 2. The design in one paragraph

Listings are public and seller-signed; **the listing is the contract**.
A buyer composes an order locally (assisted by a client-side helper),
hash-commits it with random secrets, encrypts it to the seller, and
pays a reserved pool subaddress directly from their own wallet. The
platform routes ciphertext and issues signed receipts, but in the
happy path it learns no contents, no amounts, and no confirmation that
a sale occurred. Payment verification exists **only inside disputes**.
Disputes are not a court and produce no refunds by force; they are a
sensor layer that produces a small set of hard, checkable facts about
seller failures, which — accumulated — justify warnings, restrictions,
or bans. Buyer-activated accounts cost a small one-time fee, which
prices Sybil farms and gives bans a re-entry cost.

## 3. Governing principles (owner-ratified unless marked proposed)

- **G1 — Exact privacy claim.** "Ordinary orders remain encrypted. The
  platform has no verified happy-path sales ledger. Disputes can
  disclose selected order terms and payment information.
  Communication, address-assignment, and public-listing metadata
  remain visible." Anything stronger is a false claim and must not
  appear in copy.
- **G2 — No positive trust metric.** Every submittable positive signal
  (receipts, refund counts, completion ratios, endorsements) is
  manufacturable by self-dealing at ~network-fee cost and is therefore
  excluded as a trust input. Seller records are **purely subtractive**:
  clean at start, they accumulate only findings. The one
  positive-flavoured datum is platform-*observed* continuity (time
  active with zero sustained findings), which nobody submits.
- **G3 — Denominators are one-way.** Volume figures (which are only
  estimates anyway — see T3) may soften enforcement of *service
  breaches* at human review; they may never sharpen enforcement, and
  they never mitigate *deliberate deception*.
- **G4 — Refunds are events, not currency.** A verified refund closes
  its own case's monetary claim and nothing else. It earns no credit,
  erases no finding, and buys no immunity from evidence already
  establishing misconduct.
- **G5 — The platform is a moderator with evidence, never an oracle.**
  Fulfilment and intent remain matters of evidence and judgement;
  published claims distinguish what cryptography establishes from what
  remains inconclusive.

## 4. Protocol decisions

### Setup
- **P1 — Identity.** Each account has an OpenPGP identity key.
  Registration and browsing are free (see P12 for activation).
- **P2 — The listing is the contract (owner ruling).** A listing is a
  seller-signed, versioned document: item, options, price calculation
  (integer atomic units, rounding rule, shipping calculation, any
  fiat→XMR rate with expiry), delivery and refund terms, and an
  explicit **standing-offer clause**: while this version is active,
  the seller authorizes the platform to issue address reservations
  under the listing's stated limits (eligibility, quantity bounds,
  capacity mode), and a conforming order submitted and paid within the
  stated deadlines binds the seller with no further acceptance step.
  Edits create a new signed version; superseded versions are retained
  for dispute lookback. Listings that cannot express a precise
  standing offer fall back to explicit per-order seller acceptance.
- **P3 — Pools.** Sellers generate subaddresses in their own wallet
  and upload a signed manifest. A pool signature authorizes receiving
  at those addresses; it does not prove spend-key ownership (and need
  not — a seller who uploads addresses they don't control diverts only
  their own income).
- **P4 — Reservation.** For each order, the platform atomically burns
  one pool address and issues a signed **reservation certificate**
  binding: reservation ID, buyer and seller key fingerprints, the
  address, network, issue/expiry times, and policy. Burned addresses
  are **never recycled**, even if unpaid. The certificate deliberately
  does **not** name the listing (the listing binding lives in the
  committed order and the signed listing; see P7) — the platform does
  not need to know which listing a buyer is buying, and list price
  would leak the approximate amount.
- **P5 — Reservation hygiene.** Unpaid reservations per buyer are
  capped (lower for new accounts), expiry is short, sellers are
  alerted when a pool runs low, and the seller-side tooling records
  the highest pool index issued (protecting wallet restoration beyond
  default lookahead). A payment arriving after expiry is still a
  payment: fulfil or refund — expiry ends routing privileges, not
  obligations.

### Placing an order (happy path)
- **P6 — Local composition.** The buyer's helper builds the order from
  the listing snapshot: items, quantities, prices per the listing's
  calculation rules, delivery details. The order contains a commercial
  part (items, quantities, totals, deadlines) and a separately-sealed
  shipping part. The user reviews a plain-language summary before
  anything is signed. (A determined buyer can hand-craft a
  nonconforming order; the protection is not prevention but that
  nonconforming terms do not bind the seller — assessed in a dispute,
  P10.)
- **P7 — Randomized commitments.** The submission carries split
  commitments with independent 256-bit secrets:
  C_terms = H(domain ‖ secret₁ ‖ terms), C_shipping likewise,
  C_order = H(domain ‖ C_terms ‖ C_shipping ‖ listing digest). A bare
  hash of predictable terms is not hiding; the randomizers are what
  make it a commitment.
- **P8 — Refund address in the open (owner ruling).** The buyer
  designates a refund address — a **fresh subaddress, unique per
  order** — in the signed outer submission, not inside the encrypted
  order. The buyer's signature and the platform's submission receipt
  bind together: commitment, refund address, reservation ID, network,
  and ciphertext digest; the receipt timestamps the binding.
  Rationale: a Monero address reveals nothing on-chain (outputs land
  at one-time stealth addresses); the binding prevents post-hoc
  substitution by anyone; the address can only be changed by a
  separately authenticated amendment with a defined precedence rule.
  Known limits: the signature proves designation, not wallet-key
  ownership; the platform now stores a buyer-associated destination
  (retention policy in §8).
- **P9 — Submission.** The buyer submits ciphertext + commitment +
  refund address. The platform stores the ciphertext (retained
  through the dispute window), stores the commitment immutably, and
  returns a signed submission receipt. From the platform's side this
  is an *encrypted order proposal* between two accounts — not a sale.
- **Acceptance** is standing (P2). No round-trip for standard orders.
- **Payment.** The buyer pays the reserved address from their own
  wallet, the exact amount the listing's rules produce. Acknowledgements
  ("received", "shipped") travel inside encrypted messages only, so
  **the happy path produces no platform-visible payment or completion
  confirmation.** Immediately after paying, the buyer's helper runs a
  **proof rehearsal**: generate the transaction proof while the wallet
  is open and verify it locally; store txid + **transaction private
  key** in the evidence bundle (a wallet restored from seed loses tx
  keys — without this, some buyers later *cannot* produce a proof).
- **Fulfilment.** The seller sees the payment land on the subaddress in
  their own wallet, opens the order, ships. Before shipping, the
  seller's helper checks the opened plaintext against the filed
  commitment (see P11).

### Disputes
- **P10 — Layer 1: settlement by agreement (order stays sealed).** The
  buyer opens a dispute stating the allegation (no reveal yet) and
  may sign a **settlement authorization**: case ID, order commitment,
  recorded refund address, exact amount in atomic units, deadline, and
  settlement scope. The seller may propose an amount instead; closure
  requires the *buyer's* signature on the amount — a seller cannot
  unilaterally close a case by refunding an arbitrary sum. The seller
  pays the recorded refund address; the operator verifies the payment
  evidence against the signed authorization (P13). Verified → the
  monetary claim closes as **settled without adjudication**. If the
  seller declines, doesn't respond in the window, or contests, the
  buyer may proceed to Layer 2 — silence cannot stall a case.
- **P11 — Seller's mismatch defense.** If the stored ciphertext opens
  to content that does not match the filed commitment (or is garbage),
  the seller may reveal that message's session key; the operator
  decrypts the platform-stored copy and checks it against the
  commitment, establishing buyer-side mismatch rather than seller
  non-performance.
- **P12 — Layer 2: selective disclosure.** The buyer opens C_terms
  (shipping stays sealed unless the allegation requires it). The
  operator checks: reveal matches the commitment and the submission
  receipt; terms conform to the cited signed listing version;
  reservation certificate is authentic; payment evidence (P13)
  verifies destination = reserved address and amount = listing-derived
  total; deadlines from the listing. The seller responds within a
  bounded window with their evidence (delivery records, tracking
  data, signed correspondence). Outcomes are signed, reasoned, and
  appealable: **Dismissed / Inconclusive–not established / Resolved
  mistake / Sustained service breach / Sustained deliberate deception /
  Procedural noncooperation.** Only a minimal redacted outcome is
  recorded against the seller (no txids, amounts, addresses, buyer
  identities, or evidence bundles). Refunds resolve loss; they do not
  erase independently established deception.
- **P13 — Proof qualification (applies to original payments and
  refunds).** A transaction proof authenticates specific payment
  evidence; per Monero's own documentation it does **not** guarantee
  spendability. Verification therefore checks: exact destination and
  agreed amount; confirmed inclusion with reorg handling; supported
  proof format; abnormal-output/locking conditions (e.g., timelocked
  or duplicated one-time outputs); transaction timing and prior use,
  deduplicated against previous settlement claims. Proofs are
  presented against a fresh per-case challenge (binding presentation
  to the case — noting this does not prove the transaction happened
  after the challenge). Anomalies that cannot be resolved → technical
  review or Inconclusive, never counted against a party. No UI label
  anywhere may say "verified payment" beyond what this procedure
  establishes.
- **Deliberate deception** requires named evidence kinds (e.g.,
  provably false tracking data; the seller's own signed statements
  contradicted by verified evidence), not operator intuition.

### Accounts and anti-Sybil
- **P14 — Paid activation (owner proposal).** Registration/browsing
  are free. A small one-time XMR fee activates an account for
  *transacting* (reserving, ordering, disputing). The operator issues
  a per-registration payment address (same pool machinery); activation
  on confirmation; fee is operator service revenue (trade custody is
  untouched) and is **forfeit on a fraud ban**. Sellers pay too (tier
  TBD), stacking with activation delay and a promotion-probation
  period for new sellers. The fee's primary function is a **re-entry
  cost**: bans finally cost the banned something real, and farms pay
  linearly per identity while a person pays once ever.

## 5. Trust and enforcement decisions

- **T1 — Findings-only enforcement.** Bans and restrictions rest on
  sustained findings (P12) — absolute counts of proven failures,
  weighted by severity. No finding is ever produced by an algorithm
  or a ratio.
- **T2 — Deception vs breach.** Deliberate deception (intent-grade
  evidence) is volume-independent: one sustained finding can justify
  removal at any scale, and volume never mitigates it. Service
  breaches scale with honest volume, so they are handled by graduated
  sanctions (warning → promotion restriction → suspension) with human
  review and appeal, never by raw count alone.
- **T3 — The ratio, demoted (as amended under owner attack).** The
  platform internally knows reservations issued per seller and refunds
  recorded per seller. A refunds-to-reservations figure may be used
  **only** to order the human review queue. It is never published,
  never a threshold, never a safety claim — reservations cost a
  seller ~nothing to inflate (no payment is needed to reserve), so
  any exonerating use would be manufactured safety. Counterparty
  patterns (account ages, cross-seller activity, expiry-without-event
  clusters) are likewise triage context for a human, never automated
  penalties.
- **T4 — Seller dossier (what a buyer sees).** Platform-observed
  continuity (first-seen date, key/listing continuity — key creation
  dates are not used, they're forgeable), enforcement status and any
  redacted sustained outcomes, standing "new/unestablished seller"
  state, the seller's own published fulfilment/refund terms, and a
  standing warning that direct payments have no platform-enforced
  recovery. Plus optional scoped endorsements (signed, expiring,
  revocable statements by identified parties — assertions, never a
  computed score). **No percentages, no "verified sales", no
  completion rates.**
- **T5 — Re-entry.** Banned-fingerprint blocklist (key fingerprints,
  pool addresses), paid re-activation (P14), probation on new seller
  accounts. Accepted residue: a determined operator can farm
  parallel aged identities over months; continuity is therefore shown
  as continuity, never as trading success.
- **T6 — Noncooperation is a participation rule.** Seller silence in
  disputes is enforced as procedural noncooperation (a rule breach),
  explicitly **not** as proof of non-delivery.

## 6. Usability commitments

- **U1 — The helper.** A standalone client-side tool (CLI first),
  open-source, offline-capable, releases signed and reproducibly
  built. It: generates/imports the identity key; builds orders
  deterministically from listing snapshots; creates commitments,
  signs, encrypts; verifies reservation certificates and the
  operator's receipts; runs the post-payment proof rehearsal; keeps
  the **evidence bundle** (order copy, commitment secrets, listing
  snapshot, receipts, txid + tx key, refund-wallet recovery info)
  locally, encrypted to the user's key — optionally backed up
  server-side, still encrypted to the user's key (the platform
  already knows an order record exists; the backup costs no content
  privacy, and shrinks user loss modes to "lost identity key");
  assembles dispute reveal/settlement packages. It submits nothing by
  itself — the user pastes/uploads its output — and the operator's
  root signing key is **pinned** in it. The protocol is tool-agnostic
  (public canonical spec): the helper is an aid, never a gatekeeper.
- **U2 — Happy-path effort budget (design targets).** Buyer, per
  order: choose listing → review one plain-language screen → confirm
  → pay in their own wallet → one 30-second proof rehearsal. Seller,
  per order: see payment in wallet → open order → ship. Setup, once:
  ~10 minutes with the helper. If the representative-user test (§8)
  shows these budgets blown, the design — not the user — is wrong.
- **U3 — Errors surface before payment.** Seller-side commitment
  check before shipping (P9/P11); buyer-side acceptance is standing;
  helper-built orders conform by construction. The residual
  silent-failure class we know of is *evidence loss*, mitigated by
  the bundle + backup (U1) and the refund layer working without the
  bundle (P8/P10).
- **U4 — Disputes are heavier by design, but structured.** Most
  disputes should end at Layer 1 with the order sealed. The filer's
  evidence package is assembled by the helper; the operator's review
  arrives pre-structured. Friction here is a feature (it prices
  frivolous filings), but it must price them for a *person with a
  helper*, not for an expert.

## 7. What the platform knows (summary)

Knows: accounts and key fingerprints; public signed listings and
versions; pools and reservation events (which buyer, which seller,
when, expiry); ciphertexts and commitments; refund addresses bound to
reservations; activation payments; dispute events and their redacted
outcomes.
Never knows (happy path): order contents, item quantities, totals,
shipping addresses, whether payment occurred, whether goods arrived.
Can infer (and copy must not deny): account activity timing/sizes;
that disputes correlate with real transactions.

## 8. Gates and open items we already know about

- **Representative-user workflow test is a hard gate** before this
  design is built: non-expert users must complete compose → commit →
  pay (test currency) → dispute reveal, with the helper, unaided.
- Proof-spendability adversarial cases (P13) must be validated against
  the exact wallet/proof path before any payment-denial finding can be
  auto-sustained.
- Pool restoration/lookahead must be tested (seller wallet restore
  beyond default lookahead can miss payments).
- Active key/address substitution (compromised or malicious platform
  serving attacker keys) is mitigated by helper key-pinning and
  authenticated key transitions; first-time-buyer bootstrap trust is
  a known residue.
- PGP has no forward secrecy; retained ciphertexts are a long-term
  exposure. Retention is split by class (short-lived ciphertext /
  immutable receipts / restricted dispute evidence / redacted
  enforcement history) and bans must not destroy buyers' outstanding
  evidence. (Schedule TBD — review input welcome.)
- Marketplace-id persistence, admin TOTP + separate admin listener,
  listing-image illegal-content reporting duties, clock source under
  the no-outbound rule, legal review before any public instance, and
  funding for independent security review are tracked separately.

## 9. Specific questions for reviewers

1. Does the explicit standing-offer clause (P2) create the seller
   agreement evidence that per-order acceptance was meant to create?
   Where does it still fail — eligibility, capacity, withdrawal
   mid-flight, late payment?
2. The inventory trilemma (hidden quantities / exact core-enforced
   stock / no seller involvement — pick two): is our pick (bounded
   orders + seller-managed availability + seller-borne oversell risk)
   the right two for a small marketplace?
3. Settlement-by-agreement (P10): any way for either party to game a
   signed settlement authorization — partial-scope settlements,
   multi-case dedup, amount in atomic units vs fiat drift during the
   settlement window?
4. Session-key defense (P11): is recipient disclosure of a PGP session
   key sound and sufficient here? What does it leak beyond the one
   message?
5. Refund address in the open (P8): is the correlation/retention cost
   understated anywhere? Is the authenticated-amendment procedure a
   substitution attack surface?
6. Paid activation (P14): does the fee actually change attacker
   economics at plausible price points, or does it mainly tax honest
   users? Any perverse incentive created for the operator (e.g.,
   ban-farming for fees) that needs a structural check?
7. Trust model (§5): have we smuggled a denominator back in anywhere
   (dossier, triage, continuity) in a form that can be manufactured
   into safety? Is the subtractive-only record workable as the *sole*
   buyer-facing trust story?
8. Usability (§6): which single step is most likely to lose the
   ordinary user, and what is the least-complex fix that does not
   weaken a guarantee?
9. Proof qualification (P13): is the check list complete for current
   Monero? What do FCMP++/Carrot change for subaddresses and proofs?
10. What is the strongest attack on this design as a whole that we
    have not named?
